# In-game testing with the sandbox

The `sandbox` feature spawns every item, mob and entity and removes the risk of dying, so interactions can be checked one at a time in VR and in flat play. Research and decompile references are in `docs/research/creative-test-mode.md`.

## Setup

1. Set `Sandbox.Enabled = true` in `BepInEx/config/com.thephaseless.peakxr.cfg`. It is off by default.
2. Play **offline** (Play Solo, no lobby). The sandbox refuses to act in any online lobby, your own included, so it never touches players who did not opt in.
   Once you use a sandbox action or open the console, the run saves nothing: no quicksave write or delete, no achievements, stats or ascents. A notice at the off hand (the console in flat play) says so once. Progress counts again from the next title or airport, unless god mode is still on.
3. Keys: `F1` opens the game console (unlocked by the sandbox), `F7` toggles the flat sandbox window (`Sandbox.WindowKey`; flat only, in VR it is ignored because the window shows only on the desktop and would block your input). `F5` and `Insert` belong to other mods.
4. In VR, the item room below covers the items without a keyboard. Everything else still runs from the console or the flat window; put the headset on afterwards.

## Item room

The item room (`sandbox.room`) puts every item of the game on tables in the solo airport, grouped by kind, so you can walk along and try them one after another with only the controllers.

- Quick start for testers: launch with `-peakxr-itemroom` (and `Sandbox.Enabled` on). Once the title has been up for 3 s, the room opens as if you had pressed Enter: it plays solo and builds in the airport. Started in the solo airport, it builds where you stand. In the airport it goes straight to its fixed place, so the closed spawn elevator does not matter. Only when it falls back to the measured floor does a walled-in start (under 4 m2 of open floor) measure again every second for up to 30 s. Then `Sandbox.RoomTable <kind>` in the F1 console takes you to a table.
- Enter: in the VR tab (title or pause menu), open the Sandbox page, set Enabled to On, then press Enter or leave on the Item room row under it. From the title it plays solo and builds the room once the airport has loaded; in the solo airport (Play Solo, also after the title's PLAY OFFLINE) it builds around where you stand. Flat: `Sandbox.Room` in the F1 console. Online, in a run or while loading, the button plays the refusal cue at the row and does nothing. With the sandbox off it does the same, and the log says to turn it on.
- In the solo airport the room always stands in one fixed place in the hall, wherever you press Enter: from the start line about 4 m out from the spawn elevators (world x -10, z 56.75) along the hall (world +z), on 13 x 16.75 m of floor that the Airport scene's colliders leave clear with 0.5 m to spare. If you pressed Enter elsewhere, the bell rings from the start orbs, a teal ring marks them, and the note in front of your eyes says how far away they are. Before building, it checks every table, the fire and the test wall against level geometry. If anything stands there (a game update moved the lobby), the log says so once with the first collider, and the room is placed on the measured floor as below. The log line `[sandbox.room] N items on M tables` says `the fixed airport place` or `measured room`.
- On the measured floor (when the fixed place is blocked), the room measures the open floor in 16 directions around you and puts the tables in the middle of the one that holds them best. Tables stand side by side in rows across the floor, a 1.6 m walkway between rows and past alternate row ends, so one path passes every table, at least 0.8 m from every wall and 2 m ahead of you. When that does not fit, the room tightens in steps: walkways down to 1 m and walls to 0.4 m (start walkway 1.5 m); then deeper tables (up to 1.8 m) with 10 cm between items; then 1.2 m between the areas; then walkways and area gaps all 1 m, walls 0.2 m, a 1 m start walkway and tables up to 2.4 m deep. No item is ever more than 0.7 m in from a table edge. Only then does it leave out the test wall. The game's items are game-sized (a median half a metre across, the glider 3.1 x 2.2 m), so the 139 kinds need about 15 x 15 m of open floor; the airport hall measured 14 x 18 to 20 m and keeps the wall. The eye chart stands at the far end and always stays.
- Where you stand is too tight (the spawn elevator), the room tries spots 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 m out along each heading with a clear way there, nearest first, so it lands in the airport hall outside. The notice bell then rings from the room's start orbs, a teal ring marks them, and a note in front of your eyes for 6 s says how far away it is. Floor at most a quarter too small still gets the room, with the same note saying some tables touch walls. With less floor than that the room does not open: you get the refusal cue, and a note in front of your eyes gives the size of the most open floor found and says the room needs about 15 x 15 m. If even a hall-sized floor did not hold it, the note says so instead of sending you elsewhere. Enter works again at once.
- If building or restocking the room hits an error, the room closes (or the restock stops), you get the refusal cue and the reason in front of your eyes, the error goes to `LogOutput.log` once, and Enter or the orb works again.
- Three areas, nearest first, each under a sign. Helpful: food, drink, healing, tools, climbing gear, anything that does not hurt you. Dangerous, 2.5 m behind it (1.2 m, then 1 m, on a tight floor) on darker tables: whatever kills, warps, poisons, knocks out, explodes or adds a status when used, read from the game's own item data (status changes and their sign, afflictions, and components such as warps, dynamite and the cactus ball). Goes off by itself, at the end of the dangerous row an area gap past its last table (in a row of its own when it must be kept its fuse radius away): items that act while they just lie there. Dynamite lights once you come within its fuse radius of where it lies, a beehive lets out a bee swarm that chases and poisons you, and a banana peel slips anyone who moves within 1 m of it. For dynamite, the room moves its wick out of reach until you pick it up, so it needs no more room than the other areas (only if that patch is missing does the room keep it its fuse radius plus 1 m from you, the fire, the wall and every other table). The log lists every dangerous item on the tables and why (`[sandbox.room] dangerous:`); a duplicate left out is listed only among the duplicates.
- In the room these stay quiet on their table. Dynamite stays unlit until you pick it up, then its fuse runs as usual: throw it. A banana peel slips again once you have picked it up. The beehive never gets its bees in the room.
- Items lying on a table keep their real-time lights and particle effects off (a lit torch or lantern draws the scene again for each light, in both eyes) until you pick them up. The debug line `[sandbox.room] N items placed` counts how many lights and particle systems are held off.
- When the room opens where you stand, a note in front of your eyes for 6 s says how many items and tables there are and that the orbs in front of you light the fire, restock and leave. The log line `[sandbox.room] notice up:` says how far ahead it is, how far off the view, whether the head camera draws its layer and whether it is past the near clip.
- Some item prefabs carry an `ItemOptimizer` with no item set. When one spawns, the game's optimizer turns itself off and then throws trying to unregister nothing (`ItemOptimizer.OnDisable`). With `sandbox.room` installed, that call is skipped for an unset item only.
- Within each area every kind has its own tables, so every item is within reach of a walkway: fire and light, food, medicine, climbing, throw and launch, movement, tools, mystic, bags, other. Each item gets its own footprint, from its drawn size (`ItemDrawnBox`, the meshes' real bounds; an item a game update adds is measured by its parts' boxes): 15 cm clear to the next item (10 cm on a tight floor) and 8 cm (6 cm) of table top round the edge. A long item lies a quarter turn round, its long side along the row. Every table in an area is filled to the same depth, deepest items first, and is as narrow as its items allow; a table is at least 0.6 x 0.6 m. Tables have a 7 cm top 0.88 m up on 7 cm legs, at each corner and every 1.5 m between, which cast shadows. Each item rests on the top, its lowest drawn point 4 mm above it, and lies still until you pick it up. The log line `[sandbox.room] N items on M tables` gives the block they take.
- One of each item: prefabs that share the game's display name in the same area (chess colours, guidebook pages, variants) get one spot, the shortest prefab name. A poison look-alike keeps its own spot in the dangerous area. The log gives the count (`[sandbox.room] one of each: N kinds from M items`) and the names left out.
- A table's items are only out while you are near it: they appear when you come within 4 m and those still untouched go again once you are 7 m away, so only a few tables' worth render at a time. Items you moved stay where you left them. When the room opens, every table is filled once, nearest first, while you stand at the start (about 2 s), and the far tables empty again right after: an item's first spawn of the session is the costly one, and this spreads it out where it does not matter. Spawning places one or two items a frame and stops for the frame after 2 ms. The debug log line `[sandbox.room] N items placed` names the slowest spawn of each batch.
- Also in the room: the portable stove's fire ahead of the helpful tables, for cooking and lighting. It is not there until you rest a hand in the "Light fire" orb, the white chevron left of "Restock all" in front of where you start (a "Fire" label marks where it will burn), and it goes again once you walk 8 m away; the sandbox test wall with a yellow target past the last tables on the left, for climbing, pitons, ropes and launchers; an eye chart on a white board on two posts at the far end, named over it, for the binoculars: its lower lines only read through them. The airport floor is hard, so coconuts and eggs break on it.
- Every orb in the room stands on its own post, 1.1 m above the floor, with its name just over it. The start orbs stand in a row 1 m ahead of where the room starts, 0.44 m apart: Light fire, Restock all, Leave (the cross). Rest either hand inside one, within 9 cm of its centre, until it has grown: 0.6 s for a chevron, 1 s for the cross. A faint tick says the hand is in. The log line `[sandbox.room] orbs` gives every orb's name and position.
- Restock: rest a hand in the white chevron orb on the post in front of the left corner of a kind's first table (named with the kind and its count) to put that kind back. Items still on their spot stay; items lying anywhere else in the world are removed and fresh ones placed; items in your hands or pack stay. Warp items are never removed (the game's own `WarpOnThrow` throws an error when one goes), so a thrown one stays where it landed next to its fresh copy. The chevron orb in front of where you started restocks everything. Flat: `Sandbox.Restock`.
- Leave: rest a hand in the cross orb beside it for a second, press the button again, or quit from the pause menu. Each goes back to the title. Boarding the plane closes the room and keeps the run.
- Statuses work in the room, unlike the rest of the airport, so food, medicine, poison and cold show on the bar. Once they fill 85 % of it they are cleared, and if you pass out or die anyway you are woken or revived.
- The self-test's progress and save guard (no achievements, stats, ascent completion or quicksave writes) holds while the room is open. Offline only; joining an online room closes it.
- Left out and listed in the log (`[sandbox.room] N items left out`): build previews (`*Preview`), props (`*_Prop`), constructable templates, creatures (mob items such as the beetle; spawn them from the entity list) and anything without a network view.

## Console commands

Type in the F1 console, for example `Sandbox.Spawn Rope`. Names are the prefab names (spaces optional).

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `Sandbox.Spawn <Item>` | Drop the item ahead of you |
| `Sandbox.Give <Item>` | Put the item in your hand or inventory |
| `Sandbox.SpawnAll` | Spawn every item on a grid ahead of you (the test line) |
| `Sandbox.ListItems`, `Sandbox.ListMobs` | Print names |
| `Sandbox.Mob <name>` | Spawn a mob or entity ahead of you |
| `Sandbox.ScanMobs` | Deep scan Resources for more spawnable prefabs |
| `Sandbox.God` | Toggle god mode |
| `Sandbox.Revive`, `Sandbox.Warp` | Revive where you died (passed out: where you lie); warp to spawn |
| `Sandbox.Jump <Segment>` | Beach, Tropics, Alpine, Caldera, TheKiln, Peak |
| `Sandbox.SetTime <hours>` | 0 to 24 (sunrise 5, sunset 21) |
| `Sandbox.FreeCam` | Game fly camera, flat play only |
| `Sandbox.Stamina <current> <extra>`, `Sandbox.MaxStamina <max>` | Set the stamina bar and the bonus bar; max stamina is set through Hunger |
| `Sandbox.AddStatus <Type> <amount>`, `Sandbox.ClearStatus <Type>` | Add a status the game's way (Cold, Poison, Injury...), or set one to 0 |
| `Sandbox.PassOut` | Pass out (the game's own command); `Sandbox.Revive` wakes you |
| `Sandbox.RunSetting <Setting> <value>` | Set a run setting for this run, not saved; `HelpingHand 1` is kick mode. Marks the run custom until the title screen, where the old values and flag come back |
| `Sandbox.GiveLeft <Item>`, `Sandbox.GiveRight <Item>` | Give an item into that VR hand |
| `Sandbox.Wall` | Place a climbable test wall 3 m ahead |
| `Sandbox.Room` | Open the item room (title or solo airport), or leave it |
| `Sandbox.Restock` | Put every item of the open item room back on its table |
| `Sandbox.RoomTable <kind>` | Stand in front of the first item room table whose area and kind contain `<kind>`: `Food`, `Dangerous Food`, `Goes off`, `Tools` |

The stamina, status and pass-out commands work offline only and turn god mode off first, because god mode would undo them.

The game's own commands also work, for example `Character.InfiniteStamina`, `Afflictions.AddPoison`, `Afflictions.ClearAll`, `Item.Add <Item>`.

## Procedure

Start a run, turn on god mode, then work through the sections. Record the result per item or mob in your report: pass, or what broke (log line, visual, or input).

### 1. Test line

`Sandbox.SpawnAll`, wait for "Test line done", then walk the grid. For every item:

- It spawned, has a model, and is not stuck in the ground or another item.
- Looking at it shows the right prompt; the pick-up gesture or button grabs it with the correct hand.
- Held pose is sane: grip position, orientation, nothing clipping the camera.
- Primary use and secondary use do something (see categories below), then drop and throw.
- Re-grab a thrown item. Put it in a backpack slot and take it out.

### 2. Per item category

| Category | Examples | Check in VR |
|---|---|---|
| Food and berries | Apple Berry, Berrynana, Granola Bar, Egg, TrailMix | Eat by bringing it to the mouth; button fallback; stamina or hunger change; peel or leftover spawns |
| Mushrooms | Mushroom Normie, Cluster, Glow, Poison variants | Eat effects; poison variants raise Poison; ShelfShroom and BounceShroom place and work |
| Medicine | Bandages, FirstAidKit, Antidote, Cure-All, AloeVera, Sunscreen, Heat Pack, drinks | Use on self and on a friend (`canUseOnFriend`); the matching status drops |
| Amulets and relics | Amulet_*, AncientIdol, Cursed Skull, ScoutsHonor, Strange Gem | Passive or use effect applies; Curse status behaves |
| Light and fire | Torch, Lantern, Candle, Flare, FireWood, Marshmallow, PortableStovetopItem | Lights when used; cooking changes the held item; light follows the hand |
| Climbing and rope | RopeSpool, Anti-Rope Spool, RopeShooter, ClimbingSpike, ChainShooter, RescueHook, ClimbingChalk | Aim with the hand; the rope attaches; climbing it works with arm locomotion; spike hammering |
| Movement | Balloon, Parasol, Glider, Parachute, Jetpack, Rocketpack, ScoutCannonItem, Warp Compass, WarpFungus, Warpsketball | Held item steers in VR without sickness spikes; camera fades on warps; no forced camera motion |
| Tools and misc | Compass, Binoculars, Megaphone, Bugle, Guidebook, Passport, Frisbee, Basketball, Dynamite, Snowball | Binoculars overlay is readable in VR; guidebook pages open in world space; throwables use hand velocity |
| Containers | Backpack, Fannypack, Luggage* | Open, put items in, take them out, wear on the back; luggage opens and spawns loot |
| Chess pieces (`C_*`) and props | C_King, Bishop, Knight | Grab and carry; nothing special should happen |
| Not spawnable alone | `*Preview`, `*_Prop`, `BaseConstructable*`, `TestConstructable` | Spawning is allowed; expect inert or invisible objects, do not report as bugs |

### 3. Per mob and entity

Use the Entities tab (F7) or `Sandbox.Mob <name>`. Mobs that are item prefabs (Beetle, Scorpion, Frog) appear there too.

| Name | Style | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Beetle, Scorpion, Frog | item prefab with `Mob` | Idles, notices you, attacks, ragdoll hit does not whip the VR camera; can be picked up and cooked |
| Character_Scoutmaster | room object | Chases, grabs; assumed to need its spawner link, watch the log for NREs |
| MushroomZombie | plain | Wakes near you, chases, spores status |
| BeeSwarm | plain | Chases, poisons; assumed to run without a hive |
| Tornado | needs a `TornadoSpawner` in the scene | Only spawns on a map that has one; pushes you; camera does not roll. The self-test triggers the spawner itself |
| BugfixOnYou | attaches to you | Poison ticks on you |
| RavenVoiceThief, tumbleweed | plain | Behaviour unchecked; note what it does |
| Anything else from "Deep scan" | plain | Spawns, replicates, no log errors |

For each mob: it spawns facing you, its attack applies the right status or ragdoll, god mode cancels the damage, VR hands can still grab items while it attacks, and it does not break the VR camera.

### 4. Per interaction

With god mode on, repeat each in VR and then flat:

- Grab, hold, drop, throw, and pass an item to a second hand.
- Eat, drink, use on self, use on friend, secondary use.
- Backpack in and out, hotbar slot swap, drop with a full inventory.
- Climb: wall, rope, ladder-like spike; let go and fall (god mode removes fall damage).
- Ragdoll, pass out, `Sandbox.Revive`: the camera fades or detaches; no motion sickness trigger; control returns.
- `Sandbox.Jump` to each segment and `Sandbox.Warp`: fade, rig follows the character, UI re-anchors.
- `Sandbox.SetTime` at 0, 12, 21: lighting and fog render correctly in both eyes.

## Unattended self-test

The `sandbox.selftest` feature plays the procedure above by itself, for runs where nobody watches the screen. It runs only when the game starts with `-peakxr-selftest`. For that run it turns the sandbox and god mode on without touching `Sandbox.Enabled` in the config, and it quits the game when it is done.

A self-test run grants the player's profile nothing. `SelfTestProgressGuard` blocks the four `AchievementManager` calls every unlock goes through (achievements, stat sets, stat increments and ascent completion) for the whole run, the airport included, where the game's own custom-run guard does not apply. It also skips the quicksave write a campfire light makes and the delete at the end of a run, and logs each call it blocks. When armed it records the quicksave's fingerprint (presence, size, last write, SHA-256), and the last step, `99-save-untouched`, fails unless the file still matches. `sandbox.mptest` uses the same guard.

A step that closes the grip on a held item and then uses it with the trigger waits out the closing fist first (`GripForUse`): a trigger squeezed within 0.3 s of its grip is part of the fist and no use until it opens (Core `UseTrigger.InFist`, issue #48), so a script squeezing it on the next frame never starts the use (issues #63, #64, #65, #68).

Flat: add `-peakxr-novr -peakxr-selftest` to the launch options (after the usual `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="winhttp=n,b" %command%`).

VR: start SteamVR with a headset first (a virtual one such as ALVR works, nobody has to wear it), then launch with `-force-vulkan -peakxr-selftest` and without `-peakxr-novr` (on Windows, `-force-d3d11` in place of `-force-vulkan`; see the README). The run adds the `vr.*` steps below. If VR does not start, `vr.00-session` fails with the last `[xr.bootstrap]` line, the other `vr.*` steps are `SKIP` ("VR not active") and the rest of the script runs flat.

### Running only some steps

A full VR run takes about 45 minutes. To re-check a few steps, add `-peakxr-selftest-only` and a comma-separated list of step ids. An id ending in `*` matches every id that starts with it:

```
-force-vulkan -peakxr-selftest -peakxr-selftest-only vr.173-reach-ghost
-force-vulkan -peakxr-selftest -peakxr-selftest-only "vr.230*,mob.Character_*"
```

Setup still runs, so a chosen step meets the scene, segment and god mode it has in a full run: `00-title`, `vr.00-session`, `01-airport`, `04-beach` and the `06-segment-*` jumps (`SelfTestIds.Setup`). So does the mob scan when the filter can match a `mob.` step. A few steps only mean something after another, and those run too: `vr.93b` after `vr.93` (the helicopter summon), `vr.25b` after `vr.25` (the lit fire), and `vr.86b` after the whole end-of-run slot. Every other step does its own setup (warps, the sandbox test wall, items, statuses).

Steps that are not selected are not run and not logged. The summary counts them, for example `summary: 3 pass, 0 fail, 0 skip, 474 filtered, 210s`. With exact ids only, the run stops as soon as the last one has run, and skips the remaining setup. A filter with a `*` runs to the end of the script with everything else filtered out. A run for one beach step takes about 2-4 minutes: the title, the airport and the beach load. A step in a later segment adds about 10 s per segment jump before it.

A filter that cannot match anything fails at once. A malformed list (an empty entry, or a `*` that does not end an entry) or an id or prefix that no step has fails the run before the title, naming the bad entry. The known ids are in `SelfTestIds.Known`; a Core test keeps that list in step with the runner. `-peakxr-selftest-only` is refused with `-peakxr-mptest`: each two-player step writes a checkpoint the other client answers, in order.

In a VR run each step also writes a `[selftest] frames <step>:` line to `LogOutput.log`, measured over that step alone. It has the refresh rate and frame budget, the p50, p95 and p99 frame interval, how many frames took more than 1.5 budgets, the GPU time, and the dropped and repeated frames the runtime reports (`n/a` where it reports none). `PeakXR-selftest.log` ends with a `frames:` line that counts the steps whose p95 frame ran over 1.1 budgets and names the worst one.

### Headset off and on

`-force-vulkan -peakxr-selftest -peakxr-selftest-only vr.260-headset-off` runs `vr.260` live: on the beach it logs `[selftest] vr.260-headset-off: take the headset off now` and waits up to 120 s for the runtime to report it (focus lost, an OpenXR state other than FOCUSED, or the wear sensor off), logs `headset off seen (...); put it back on`, then waits up to 600 s for all of them back. The step line lists every focus source and wear sensor reading it saw, which is what the runtime reported. With SteamVrMcp driving the virtual headset:

1. `launch_app` PEAK with the options above, then `wait_for_log` for `vr\.260-headset-off: take the headset off now` (timeout 600).
2. `set_proximity` with `on_head: false`, then `wait_for_log` for `headset off seen` (timeout 120). A timeout here means the runtime reported nothing; the step fails saying so.
3. Leave it off for a few seconds, or past SteamVR's standby timeout to see whether the runtime then stops the session. `screenshot` with source `compositor` shows what the runtime got meanwhile.
4. `set_proximity` with `on_head: true`, then `wait_for_log` for `vr\.260-headset-off (PASS|FAIL|SKIP)` (timeout 600).
5. Read `PeakXR-selftest.log` and the `[xr.bootstrap]` lines in `LogOutput.log`: `OpenXR session X -> Y` for each state change and `Headset worn|taken off` for the wear sensor.

### Live input

Launch with `-force-vulkan -peakxr-selftest -peakxr-selftest-hold` (VR, SteamVR running). The script runs `00-title`, `vr.00` to `vr.03` (`vr.02c` reports SKIP), `01-airport`, `04-beach`, `vr.05`, `vr.06`, `vr.231`, `vr.277`, `vr.278`, `vr.470`, `vr.471`, `vr.279`, `04b-bug-report`, `vr.08`, `vr.08b`, `vr.281`, `vr.282`, `vr.09`, `vr.33` and `vr.247` with the real controllers, skips the tutorial, settings, items, use, mob and segment steps, writes `PeakXR-selftest.log` and then leaves the game running on the beach. It quits 20 minutes later. In that time you, or a SteamVR driver with virtual controllers, can drive the controllers.

With `-peakxr-selftest` (hold or not) every VR sample is diffed and changes are logged to `BepInEx/LogOutput.log` (flushed per line), one line per edge:

- `[input] L|R tracked|primary|secondary|menu|stickclick|triggertouch|primarytouch on|off`
- `[input] L|R trigger|grip level N (0.62)`: N is 0 below 0.1, 1 below 0.5, 2 below 0.9, 3 from 0.9.
- `[input] L|R stick-right|stick-left|stick-up|stick-down on|off (x,y)`: crossing 0.5.
- `[input] L|R pos x y z`: at most once per second, after the hand moved more than 2 cm.
- `[input] char jumpWasPressed|sprintIsPressed|usePrimaryIsPressed|useSecondaryIsPressed|interactIsPressed|dropIsPressed|emoteIsPressed|pingWasPressed|move-right|move-left|move-forward|move-back on|off`: the local `CharacterInput` after all bindings ran.

Look for: each `L|R` line you drove has a matching `char` line in the same frame range (stick up gives `move-forward`, trigger level 3 gives `usePrimaryIsPressed` while holding an item). An `L|R` line with no `char` line means the binding dropped it; no `L|R` line means the sampler never saw it.

The script:

| Step id | Checks |
|---|---|
| `00-title` | The Title scene and `MainMenu` load |
| `01-airport` | Play Solo goes offline and loads the Airport with a local character |
| `02-settings-vr` | The pause menu opens, its settings page shows the VR tab, and the menu closes again. In VR the VR tab must show its grid (not one column), and no row of any rail entry's first page, or of the second page of the longest entry, may hang below or past the settings window, and no action button's caption may end cut short (the Item room button once read "ENTE~"); aimed shots of the Comfort page, that second page and a preview opening beside a left-column row |
| `item.<name>` | Every `ItemDatabase` item spawns in rows of 8, 2.5 m ahead, and two frames later still exists, has its `Item` component and at least one enabled collider (without one a hand could never grab it); a screenshot per row (`03-items-<row>`) |
| `use.<name>` | One item each of Granola, Apple, Compass, Pirate Compass, Binoculars, RopeSpool, Lantern, Flare, Torch, RopeShooter and ChainShooter, picked by exact name first (`Compass`, not `Cheat Compass`, whose use warps to Caldera); `SKIP` when no item name matches. The item is given to the hand, must still be there 0.5 s later, and is used for its own use time (`Item.usingTimePrimary`, plus the margin below) or until its cast finishes: the game must start its primary use (`Item.isUsingPrimary`) or use the item up. An item that leaves the hand counts as used only when the use destroyed it (eaten, spent, turned into another item) or logged it as consumed (`lastConsumedItem`); one dropped whole fails. The use is the player's: in VR the holding hand's trigger through the scripted controller, a mouth item (EatAtMouth on) brought to the mouth as in vr.12, and an item whose bare trigger is refused (commune, read) held at its face; flat play presses the use button. #### Spatial UI

Each step turns its `UI.*` setting on for the step and puts it back afterwards, also after a timeout, so a turned-off feature can no longer pass without being tested. A text check (`Readable`, rule in `PeakXR.Core.Legibility`) reads the text where the game draws it. The text has to be shown, not faded (alpha 0.5 or more) and not empty. Seen from the eye it has to be at least 0.25 m away, at most 45 degrees off the gaze, at most 60 degrees edge-on, and at least 0.4 degrees tall, the smallest legible size in Microsoft's mixed-reality typography guidance. Labels at a hand are judged with the gaze on that hand, and everything else with the gaze along the head's view. The note gives the measured size, distance and angles. The screenshots re-aim the camera (`ShotAt`), so a frame proves a label was drawn, not that the player could see it. That is decided from poses.

Steps `vr.10` to `vr.20` need no one at the controllers. While each one runs, `VRInput.TestOverride` replaces both controllers right after the sampler with scripted hands, which rest tracked in front of the chest with a finger on the trigger. The step moves a hand, closes a grip or pushes a stick, and the rest of the mod treats that as real controller input. Each step starts with empty hands and an empty hotbar, and from the same stance (`Settle`, `SelfTestRunner.Settle.cs`): grips, triggers and sticks released, any climb ended, the body back on this segment's home spot (flat, open ground found by the first hand step there, else the game's base camp spawn) facing the home heading, grounded, under control, the rig recentred and its eye guard waited out, then the hip's net horizontal travel within 3 cm over 2 s, each end averaged over 1 s so the game's ~1 s idle sway cancels while a walk, push or creep does not (`StillWindow`, shared with the MP settle), with the view's eye guard done (`RigDriver.ViewSettling`). Before that, a dead, passed-out or skeleton body is restored to a normal living one with its statuses cleared, and loose items within 1.5 m of the feet are removed. When hip drift alone kept the window from closing (a revive still waking or the view still in its eye guard does not count), the body crept off the home spot by itself, so the settle drops that spot and finds flat, open ground nearby instead of warping back onto it. A body that does not settle fails the step with "not settled".

Scripted hands stay within a player's reach. A hand may sit at most 1.00 m from the eye (eye to shoulder plus a stretched arm), with 5 mm of slack, and a step whose pose goes past it fails with the hand's distance and the world positions behind the verdict. For a low point the head stoops as a player squats, keeping the eye 0.75 m above it, down to a 0.9 m drop; the reach is measured from that stooped eye. A prop dropped for a stooped grab is first left to stop rolling (up to 2 s). One that rests closer than 0.35 m or farther than 0.7 m from the eye, level, is set down on the ground 0.55 m ahead (`RestWithinReach`), as a player would step up to it.

When a step ends, the grips open, the override is cleared and anything the step dropped or threw is removed, so a failed step leaves nothing behind for the next one.

Refusal steps check the whole cue the player gets as well as the refusal. ui.affordance writes each refusal or notice into a small capture as it shows it: the reason, the ring drawn (glyph, angular size, red tint), the sound and the haptic pulses per hand (`CueLog`, `CueCapture`). `ExpectCue` (`SelfTestRunner.Cues.cs`) fails a step unless the reason, a ring of at least `CueLayout.MinDegrees`, the refused (or notice) sound and a delivered haptic pulse all reach that hand in time. With `tint` it also needs the red preview on the refused thing. The ring, or its in-view edge copy when the spot is outside the view cone, must also sit wholly inside the headset's view from the eye. Players look at the hand they act with, so a step that grabs, eats, uses, places or cooks with a hand in front of the body first turns the scripted head toward that hand, or along its pointing ray for an aimed item or a ping (`LookAtHand`). A laser press or a kiosk step turns it toward that world point instead (`LookAtPoint`). The turn follows `HeadGlance`: the head covers all but the last 20 degrees (a quarter of a smaller turn), within 60 degrees up or down and 70 to either side, easing in over about 0.3 s, and the view is judged once the turn is within 3 degrees of its goal. A step whose hand is out of sight (over the shoulder, behind the back, busy climbing, or a body action such as a jump, sprint or spectator switch) passes `seen: false`: the view check is skipped and the sound and the haptic carry the cue. A pulse withheld because `Interaction.Haptics` is off counts as delivered. `ExpectFeel` checks the cues that are not refusals (progress, contact, cancel, end stop, danger). `ExpectNoCue` fails a step that should stay quiet when that hand gets a reason, ring or pulse. `vr.60` to `vr.65`, the spool's NoWall press and `vr.46`'s stamina refusal use `ExpectCue`. A tie that works uses `ExpectNoCue`. When no wall turns up anywhere, `vr.15`, `vr.44` and `vr.46` use the sandbox test wall.

Each step has a timeout. A step fails when its action fails, when it times out, or when the game logs an exception while it runs. Errors the game logs are written next to the step but do not fail it. A patch error that PeakXR's patch guard catches (`PatchGuard`, which keeps it out of the game) fails the running step the same way. A feature such an error degraded also gets a failing `compat.<feature id>` line at the end of the run, so errors between steps or during a scene load still fail it.

Results:

- `BepInEx/PeakXR-selftest.log`: one line per step (`id PASS|FAIL|SKIP 1.23s note | first logged error`); a step that skips keeps its first logged error, and one that logged a PeakXR exception before skipping fails instead. Then the totals and the `[compat]` feature states. Lines are appended as steps finish, so a crash still leaves the steps run so far.
- `BepInEx/PeakXR-selftest/*.png`: screenshots named after the steps (`03-items-000.png`, `05-mob-BeeSwarm.png`) at the game window's resolution. Old screenshots are deleted when a new run starts.
- `BepInEx/LogOutput.log` has the same step lines with a `[selftest]` prefix.

## Two-client multiplayer test

This run covers part of the live test plan in `docs/multiplayer-compat.md` without anyone at the keyboard. Client A is PeakXR in VR on one Steam account. Its hands are scripted through `VRInput.TestOverride`, the same way as the self-test's `vr.*` steps. Client B is a second Steam account with its own copy of the game (on one Linux machine: a second Steam install such as Flatpak Steam, or another OS user), running vanilla PEAK plus one test plugin, `MpObserver`, that watches A and acts only on B's own character. The two accounts must be Steam friends.

B hosts a lobby through the title menu's Host button and writes `lobby.json`. A joins that lobby through the game's own `SteamLobbyHandler.TryJoinLobby`, then plays a scripted sequence. After each step A appends a line to `a-checkpoints.jsonl`. B answers each line with its own snapshot of A's character under the same name. A sends nothing beyond what PeakXR always sends. B sends no custom RPC or event, sets no property and never calls anything on A's character; it uses only the game's own methods on its own character, as a player could, and only when A asks (requests below).

### Shared directory

Both clients read and write one directory, `$MP_DIR` below. Pass it to both with `-mpdir`. When the two clients run on one Linux machine with B in Flatpak Steam, pick a folder under Flatpak Steam's own data folder in your home: Flatpak's sandbox hides the rest of the home and has a private `/tmp`, but it mounts that folder at the same host path, so native Steam and a direct Proton launch see it too. Without `-mpdir`, both plugins fall back to `peakxr-mp` in that Flatpak folder (`MpTestFiles.MpDir.DefaultUnderHome`), taking the home from `$HOME` or, when Wine hides `HOME`, from Wine's `WINEHOMEDIR`. Each side deletes its own old files when it starts: A deletes `a-*`, and B deletes `b-*` and `lobby.json`. They only do that in a folder the test made itself (it leaves a `.peakxr-mp` marker there) or the default one, never in a drive root or your home folder; anywhere else they log a warning and leave the old files, so point `-mpdir` at a new, empty folder. Old files cannot spoil a run either way: B starts over in a checkpoint or request file whose first line changed (Core `LogCursor`), and A takes only an answer written after its request.

### Client B setup (once)

B's game folder, `$B_DIR` below, must be on the same build as A (`version.txt`). Give it the BepInEx loader and MpObserver only, never PeakXR or its preloader. From the repo root, with `$PEAK_DIR` as A's game folder (which has BepInExPack_PEAK):

```sh
mise run build-observer
cp "$PEAK_DIR"/{winhttp.dll,doorstop_config.ini,.doorstop_version} "$B_DIR"/
mkdir -p "$B_DIR/BepInEx/plugins/MpObserver"
cp -r "$PEAK_DIR/BepInEx/core" "$B_DIR/BepInEx/"
cp dist/MpObserver/MpObserver.dll "$B_DIR/BepInEx/plugins/MpObserver/"
```

Under Proton, BepInEx only loads when Wine prefers the native `winhttp.dll`. Set B's launch options in its Steam to `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="winhttp=n,b" %command% -mpobs -mpdir <MP_DIR>`, with `<MP_DIR>` written out in full. With the flags in the launch options, a plain `-applaunch` needs no arguments. MpObserver stays idle on any launch without `-mpobs`. Instead of the DLL override you can add `"winhttp"="native,builtin"` under `[Software\\Wine\\DllOverrides]` in the `user.reg` of B's Proton prefix (`steamapps/compatdata/3527290/pfx` in B's Steam library). Launch PEAK once first so that the prefix exists, and edit the file only while the game is closed.

Check the setup by launching B once. `BepInEx/LogOutput.log` in B's game folder must list `MpObserver` and nothing from PeakXR.

MpObserver puts B under the same progress and save guard as the self-test: B earns no achievements or stats, completes no ascent, and never writes or deletes its quicksave. The log names each call it blocks. If a guarded game method is missing, the observer refuses to start. It records a fingerprint of B's quicksave at the start and at the end, and `tools/mp-check.sh` fails when the two differ. On A, the progress guard is `SelfTestProgressGuard`: the self-test ends with `99-save-untouched` and the MP script with `save-untouched`.

### Launch

Start B first, before SteamVR, because Steam can block game launches while SteamVR runs (the VR Theater prompt). With B in Flatpak Steam, for example:

```sh
flatpak run com.valvesoftware.Steam -applaunch 3527290 -mpobs -mpdir "$MP_DIR"
```

Leave out the flags when they are already in the launch options. `steam://run/3527290` also works, but Steam asks for confirmation when that URL carries arguments, and nobody is there to click. B waits for the title, switches the lobby type to Friends (A joins by id), presses Host, and writes `lobby.json` once the Airport room is ready.

Then start SteamVR with a headset (a virtual one works) and launch A with `-force-vulkan -peakxr-mptest -mpdir "$MP_DIR"` (in PeakXR's usual launch options, see the README). To skip Steam's launch prompt on Linux, launch PEAK through Proton directly. `$PROTON` is the Proton build Steam runs PEAK with, and `$STEAM_DIR` is A's Steam folder:

```sh
cd "$PEAK_DIR" && \
STEAM_COMPAT_DATA_PATH="$STEAM_DIR/steamapps/compatdata/3527290" \
STEAM_COMPAT_CLIENT_INSTALL_PATH="$STEAM_DIR" \
SteamAppId=3527290 SteamGameId=3527290 \
WINEDLLOVERRIDES="winhttp=n,b" \
"$PROTON/proton" run "$PEAK_DIR/PEAK.exe" -force-vulkan -peakxr-mptest -mpdir "$MP_DIR"
```

`-peakxr-mptest` cannot be combined with `-peakxr-selftest`. A waits up to 4 minutes for a `lobby.json` written in the last 30 minutes. Without VR, the steps marked VR below are `SKIP`.

A quits after writing `a-done.json`. B quits when it sees that file, 10 minutes after hosting if nobody joined, or once A is quiet: at the later of 15 minutes after A joined and 5 minutes after A's last checkpoint (`MpRunLimit`; #103: B left a slow run with checkpoints to go). A step that fails or skips drops what A still holds before the hands move (#100: leftover food fed B on the next walk in). After every VR step A that stands more than 5 m from B walks back to B (up to 45 s), so one long walk does not strand the rest of the script.

| `save-untouched` | Checks A's quicksave against its fingerprint from when `SelfTestProgressGuard` armed; FAIL if the guard is not armed or the save changed. Then A wakes itself | The usual snapshot; mp-check compares B's own quicksave fingerprints in `b-summary.json` |

Before `join`, A runs `mp.title` (waits up to 180 s for the title menu) and, in VR, `vr.00-session`. Neither writes a checkpoint.

`scene-button` (VR) runs after `ping` and before `board`: A grips a scene button only the host can press (a host-owned trigger) and expects the Blocked cue, and B must see no `RPCA_Trigger`. The social checkpoints from `feed-refuse` on run after `feed`, and `spectate` is last. Between them A asks B to wake when B is passed out, dead or struggling.

A step that finds nothing to do (no wall or no food within 40 m, no host-owned scene button, no pack on B) is `SKIP`. `kick-mode` is also `SKIP` unless the host run has kick mode on (`HelpingHand`); `Sandbox.RunSetting` sets it only offline and for that run, so it cannot carry into a hosted lobby. Run 4 (one VR client, one unmodded client, before the social and world checkpoints) gave 16 pass, 0 fail, 2 skip (`eat` and `feed`, no food in range), and `tools/mp-check.sh` printed no `FAIL`.

### Observer requests

B obeys requests from A. A appends `{id, do, t}` lines to `a-requests.jsonl`; B ignores lines older than the lobby, runs each one and appends `{id, do, ok, note, t}` to `b-acks.jsonl` (and a `request` line to `b-observer.jsonl`). A waits 5 s for the ack by default, and a missing or failed ack skips the step. Every request uses only the game's own methods on B's own character, the calls a vanilla player triggers by playing:

| Request | Vanilla calls on B's own character | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `passout` | `Character.PassOut` (the game's console command: `CharacterAfflictions.Starve`, then the `RPCA_PassOut` RPC on B's view); `CharacterAfflictions.AddStatus(Injury, 1)` when a NoHunger buff refused the hunger | carry and spectate need a friend who is down and stays down |
| `web` | `CharacterAfflictions.AddStatus(Web, 0.6)`, as a spider's web does; refused in the airport | the help-up reach needs a friend in trouble |
| `wake` | B's own crouch input released, `CharacterAfflictions.ClearAllStatus`, then the `RPCA_ReviveAtPosition` RPC on B's view where it lies, when dead or passed out | puts B back on its feet between steps |
| `crouch`, `stand` | B's own crouch input (`CharacterInput.crouchIsPressed`) | a pal jump from below; no current step sends them |
| `stand-on` | the `WarpPlayerRPC` RPC on B's view, onto A's head | the pal jump needs B standing on A |
| `hang-below` | the `WarpPlayerRPC` RPC on B's view, onto the face found by stepping out (to 4 m) along the request's direction (else A's look) from the request's lip point (else the ground under A) to the first 1.5-5 m drop, then casting back toward A | the reach-out over a ledge needs B on the face below |
| `start-climb` | the `StartClimbRpc` RPC on B's view (`CharacterClimbing.cs:349`) on the face within 1.2 m toward A | B climbing below A is a friend to help up |
| `wear-pack` | B, the master, spawns a backpack at its feet with `PhotonNetwork.InstantiateItemRoom` (as the game's spawners do) and 0.3 s later wears it with the backpack wheel's own `Backpack.Wear` (`BackpackWheel.cs:133`): `Item.Interact` sends `RequestPickup`, and `Player.AddItem` fills B's backpack slot and syncs it. Nothing when B already wears one | `pack`, `vr.330-pack-stash` and `vr.434-point-friend-pack` need a friend wearing a pack, which a vanilla host never is |
| `spawn-item` | B, the master, spawns the named item (`item` in the request) with `PhotonNetwork.InstantiateItemRoom` 0.7 m in front of A along the request's direction, 0.3 m over the ground there | `eat`, `two-hand`, `feed`, `feed-refuse`, `vr.423-reach-out-hands-full`, `vr.330-pack-stash` and `vr.434-point-friend-pack` need an item at hand; fetching one walked A up to 175 m off (#103) |

B never sends a custom RPC or event, never sets a room or player property, and never calls anything on A's character. `tools/mp-check.sh` keeps B's own calls apart from A's traffic: the RPC lines carry `local` (true for B's own character) and every A check filters on `local` false; the event counts and unknown-event errors in `b-summary.json` count what B received, and Photon does not deliver B's own RPCs back to B as events.

### Files

All files are in the shared directory.

- `lobby.json`: `{lobbyId, hostSteamId, time, room, peakVersion, game}` from B.
- `a-checkpoints.jsonl`: one line per checkpoint with `status` (pass, fail or skip), `note`, A's `pos`, `head`, `look`, held item (`heldId`), `itemId`, `sent151`, and per step `landing`, `ping`, `landingToHost` or the join fields (`versionOk`, `inLobby`, `masterIsHost`, `lobbyVersion`, `localVersion`).
- `a-mptest.log`: the self-test style step log. `a-done.json`: A's reason for ending, totals, and counts of the events it sent by code.
- `b-observer.jsonl`: `start`, `lobby`, `join`, a `sample` of every remote character every 0.5 s (position, head, look, current item and its state, use flags, climbing, carrying, carried, passed out, fall time, hand IK targets, events received), `checkpoint` lines (a sample plus ground items and the RPCs since the previous checkpoint), every observed `rpc`, and `log` lines for exceptions, errors and unknown-event errors.
- `b-summary.json`: end reason, received event counts by code (`event151` must be 0), RPC counts, exceptions with the first 20 messages, errors, and unknown-event errors.
- `a-<checkpoint>.png` and `b-<checkpoint>.png`: each side's screen at the checkpoint. B turns its view toward A first.

### Checking the result

```sh
tools/mp-check.sh [--modded-b] [mp-dir]   # POS_TOL=1.0, LAND_TOL=1.5 metres, LAG_TOL=1.0 s, HOLD=2.0 s by default; --modded-b: see "Modded B"
```

It prints one `PASS`, `FAIL`, `SKIP` or `INFO` line per check and exits with 1 when any check fails:

- B saw a remote player join.
- A's version check passed: the lobby's version equals A's, A is in the lobby, and the master is the host.
- A wrote a checkpoint for every step of its script, finished it (`a-done.json` says `script done`), ran in VR and logged no failed step.
- For each checkpoint A passed: B logged it with A present within `HOLD` seconds (A's still hold after a checkpoint), B's view of A's position is within `POS_TOL`, and B sees the same held item id as A. B can write its checkpoint before A's equip RPC arrives, so the first matching sample within `LAG_TOL` seconds after the checkpoint counts. For `drop` and `throw`, an item of that kind lies within `LAND_TOL` of where A saw it land. A checkpoint A failed is a `FAIL`, and one A skipped is a `SKIP`.
- B logged no exceptions and no unknown-event errors, and received no event 151.
- A sent no event 151 (with a vanilla host, net.pose has no peers).
- What B received for a step comes from its own `rpc` lines between its previous checkpoint and this one plus `LAG_TOL`. A check that something must not arrive looks on until B's next checkpoint, so a late RPC still counts.
- `walk-to-host` and `regroup`: B sees A within 1.5 m plus `POS_TOL`. `emote`: B got A's `RPCA_PlayRemove` for the emote A hovered. `ping`: B got A's `ReceivePoint_Rpc` within `LAND_TOL` of A's point. `board`: B is on A's level. `luggage`: B saw an `OpenLuggageRPC` for each case A opened. `drop` and `throw`: B got A's `DropItemRpc`. `eat`: B saw A's `Consume`. `holster`: B saw an `EquipSlotRpc` stow, then a draw. `climb`: B got A's `StartClimbRpc`. `feed`: B got A's `SendFeedDataRPC` and its own `GetFedItemRPC`.
- `feed-refuse`: B got no `GetFedItemRPC` or `SendFeedDataRPC`. `reach-deny`: B got no local `RPCA_Fall`. `scene-button`: no `RPCA_Trigger` reached B. `campfire-far`: B saw no `Light_Rpc`.
- `kick-mode`: B logged a local `RPCA_Fall` from the shove. `carry`: B logged a remote `RPCA_StartCarry` and, at least 0.5 s later, `RPCA_Drop`. `pal-jump`: B logged a local `JumpRpc` with `isPalJump`. `spectate`: B's checkpoint shows A passed out. `reach`: B's own Web status is 0.05 or less and B's own `sinceGrabFriend` is 5 s or less, so the reach caught and freed B. `pack`: B wore a pack before the step (or was asked to put one on, a `wear-pack` request line before the checkpoint), still wears it and got no `RPCAddItemToCharacterBackpack` before the checkpoint (the next window holds the stash on purpose). `vr.330-pack-stash` and `vr.434-point-friend-pack`: B got `RPCAddItemToCharacterBackpack`. `vr.421-reach-out-cone`, `vr.422-reach-out-idle` and `vr.423-reach-out-hands-full`: B is still webbed (Web over 0.05). `vr.424-reach-out-far`: as `reach`. `vr.420-reach-out-ledge`: B's own `sinceGrabFriend` is 5 s or less.
- B's quicksave fingerprint is the same at the end as at the start.

`INFO` lines list the skipped checkpoints and any emote B saw from A outside the `emote` step. The remaining checks are by eye: the screenshots for how A's held items and arms look on B's screen, and the hand IK targets for `two-hand`.

### Modded B: PeakXR without a headset

The default run pairs A with a vanilla B. This second mode gives B PeakXR as well, running flat, next to MpObserver. It covers two cases the vanilla run cannot:

- A modded player with no XR. With `VRSession.Active` false, B must play like vanilla: every VR-only feature skipped, no exceptions, a normal mono camera, and no event 151 sent.
- The receive half of VR to VR. B publishes the `PeakXR` handshake property (`Net/NetPoseSync.cs`), receives A's event 151, passes it through `RemotePoseStream.Accept` (`src/PeakXR.Core/PeerTable.cs`: 40/s per sender, burst 10, parts within 4 m of the hip) and drives A's avatar with A's head and hands (rows 1 to 3 and 9 of `docs/multiplayer-compat.md`).

PeakXR already has a flat switch. With the preloader's `Runtime.Mode = Disabled` (or the launch option `-peakxr-novr`), the preloader sets `PEAKXR_RUNTIME=Disabled` and installs no XR runtime files. The plugin then skips every feature with `VROnly` and never creates the XR bootstrap, so XR never starts. The flat-safe features stay on: `net.pose`, `avatar.local`, `avatar.emotes`, `ui.settings` and `sandbox`. Leaving `Mode` at `OpenXR` on a machine without a headset is a different path: the VR features install, the bootstrap tries OpenXR and logs an error when nothing starts, and that error would fail the check below. B gets `Mode = Disabled` in its config file, so its Steam launch options stay as they are.

B keeps its usual launch options from the vanilla setup (`-mpobs -mpdir <MP_DIR>`, plus `-force-vulkan` under Proton). Install with B's game closed, from the repo root. When B is another OS user, copy into `$B_DIR` as that user, so the files stay B's:

```sh
mise run package && mise run build-observer
BX="$B_DIR/BepInEx"
rm -rf "$BX/plugins/PeakXR" "$BX/patchers/PeakXR"
cp -r dist/PeakXR/BepInEx/plugins/PeakXR "$BX/plugins/"
cp -r dist/PeakXR/BepInEx/patchers/PeakXR "$BX/patchers/"
printf '[Runtime]\nMode = Disabled\n' > "$BX/config/PeakXR.Preloader.cfg"
cp dist/MpObserver/MpObserver.dll "$BX/plugins/MpObserver/"
```

Launch both sides as usual, B first. After the run:

```sh
tools/mp-check.sh --modded-b "$MP_DIR"
```

B's `BepInEx/LogOutput.log` should show `PEAKXR_RUNTIME=Disabled` and `[compat] xr.bootstrap Skipped (VR disabled)`.

When PeakXR is loaded on B, the observer records more. It still never references PeakXR: it finds PeakXR's types by name and reads them through reflection and read-only Harmony postfixes.

- A `peakxr` line at start: PeakXR's version, `PEAKXR_RUNTIME`, the feature report (`FeatureManager.Features`) and how many game methods each feature patches.
- A `handshake` line whenever A's or B's `PeakXR` property changes (raw ints: protocol in the low 16 bits, bit 16 set while in VR).
- In every sample and checkpoint: `vrDriven` (A's avatar has an `AvatarPoseDriver` with a fresh pose), `acc151` (packets accepted so far), and A's avatar `boneHead`, `boneL` and `boneR` in A's body frame (hip rigidbody, look yaw; `MpPoseFrame` in `src/PeakXR.Core/MpPoseCheck.cs`).
- In `b-summary.json`: `sent151` and `sentEvents` (what B raised, from a `PhotonNetwork.RaiseEvent` prefix that only counts), `xrEverActive` and `stereoEver`, `bepinexErrors` with the first messages (errors and fatals from any BepInEx log source except Unity's own log), and a `peakxr` block. That block holds `vrEverActive`, PeakXR's warnings, the feature report and `pose151`: packets received, the peak in any 1 s window, the mean over the seconds that had packets (`RateWindow`), and the `RemotePoseStream.Accept` verdicts (Accepted, RateLimited, Malformed, OutOfOrder).

The observer writes `sent151`, `xrEverActive`, `stereoEver`, `bepinexErrors` and `peakxrLoaded` in the vanilla mode too. In that mode, `mp-check.sh` fails when `peakxrLoaded` is true, which catches an incomplete switch back.

A's checkpoints carry `poseHead`, `poseL` and `poseR` while its arms follow the tracked pose: the tracked head and the wrist targets the tracked hands ask for (`AvatarPoseDriver.HandTarget`). Next to them are `boneHead`, `boneL` and `boneR`, A's own avatar bones, all in the same body frame.

`--modded-b` keeps every vanilla check (positions, held items, landings, step RPCs, exceptions, quicksave) and swaps "B received no event 151" and "A sent no event 151" for:

- B loaded PeakXR, ran with `PEAKXR_RUNTIME=Disabled`, and never had a VR session, an XR device or a stereo camera.
- No PeakXR feature failed, every skipped one says `VR disabled` or `not requested`, and `xr.bootstrap` is skipped. An `INFO` line lists the active features and their patch counts.
- B published a flat handshake, saw A's VR handshake, and both carry the same protocol.
- B received 151 from A and PeakXR accepted some. None was malformed. The mean rate over the seconds with packets lies between `RATE_MIN` and `RATE_MAX` (2 and 30 per second: the 0.25 s keepalive and `PoseRate.MaxRate`). B received at least half of what A sent. The peak in 1 s and the rate-limited and out-of-order counts are `INFO`, because B's frame hitches (screenshots, level loads) bunch packets on arrival, and the bucket limiting them is the intended behaviour.
- At `walk-to-host`, `ping`, `regroup`, `grab`, `pickup`, `holster` and `two-hand` (A stands still with tracked arms), B drives A's avatar, and its hand bones lie within `HAND_TOL` (0.25 m) of A's tracked wrist targets. The line also gives the distance to A's own hand bones and head bone. At least one of these checkpoints must pass.
- B sent no event 151 and logged no PeakXR or BepInEx errors.
- A sent event 151.

Switch B back to vanilla, with B's game closed (again as B's user). This removes PeakXR's plugin, preloader, both config files and the preloader's state file, and keeps MpObserver and BepInEx:

```sh
rm -rf "$B_DIR/BepInEx/plugins/PeakXR" "$B_DIR/BepInEx/patchers/PeakXR" \
  "$B_DIR/BepInEx/config/com.thephaseless.peakxr.cfg" "$B_DIR/BepInEx/config/PeakXR.Preloader.cfg" \
  "$B_DIR/PEAK_Data/PeakXR.Preloader.state"
ls "$B_DIR/BepInEx/plugins" "$B_DIR/BepInEx/patchers"
```

The listing must show only `MpObserver` under plugins and nothing under patchers. With `Mode = Disabled`, the preloader never put XR files into `PEAK_Data`. If B ever ran PeakXR with another mode, run it once more with `Mode = Disabled` before removing it, so the preloader takes its files out of `PEAK_Data/Plugins/x86_64` and `PEAK_Data/UnitySubsystems`. The next vanilla `tools/mp-check.sh` fails if PeakXR still loaded on B.

This mode tests one direction with one sender. A second headset is still needed for B in VR: B sending poses, A receiving and applying them, both sides' rate limits under two-way traffic, and the send rate scaling of row 9, which only drops below the configured 25 Hz with four or more VR players (250/N² Hz).

## What the sandbox does not cover

- A vanilla client or someone else's lobby: the sandbox is inactive there on purpose.
- Mobs and hazards that exist only inside map prefabs (spiders, jellyfish, lava, geysers): reach them by `Sandbox.Jump` to the right segment.
- Free camera in VR: refused.
