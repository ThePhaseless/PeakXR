# VR controls

The default layout for a right-handed player. With `Input.DominantHand = Left` the two controllers swap roles.

| Control | Left controller | Right controller |
|---|---|---|
| Stick | Move | Left/right: turn. Up/down: next or previous item |
| Stick click | Tap: sprint | Crouch |
| A / X (lower button) | Tap: put the item away or take it out again. Hold: take out the backpack | Jump |
| B / Y (upper button) | Tap: ping. Hold: emote wheel | Drop. Hold to charge a throw |
| Grip | Grab or interact | Grab or interact |
| Trigger | Use (secondary) | Use (primary) |
| Menu | Release within 1 s: pause. Hold 1 s: recenter | Same as left |
| Both stick clicks together | Hold 1 s: recenter. Keep holding 1.5 s more: save a bug report | (one chord) |

Notes:

- Ropes and vines: grip one to grab it, then pull hand over hand to climb along it (`Interaction.RopeHandOverHand`). Open both hands for longer than `ClimbRegripGrace` to let go; jump and the stick still work.
- Pitons and pickaxe handles: grip the handle to hang from it, and open the hand to let go (`Interaction.PitonGripHang`). With the setting off, interact again or jump to let go. One-handed play lets go of ropes, vines and handles the same way.
- Switching items with the stick, putting one away with X or taking out the backpack with a held X does nothing while you climb, hang on a rope, use an item, hold a sticky item or are otherwise locked from switching, as in the game. The pressing hand gets the refusal cue with the reason.
- Hammering a piton: holding use places it in the game's own time. Striking the piton as it goes in drives it in up to a third sooner, with a knock at each strike (`Interaction.Tools.HammerSpikes`).
- As in the game, pitons go in only from the first belt slot that holds one. Draw one from a later slot and press use, and you get the refusal cue with a ring at the slot that works.
- Wall climbing: grip rock to hold on, then move a gripping hand to move yourself. Pull it down to climb up, pull it sideways to move across, and push it up to climb down. You travel as far as your hand did. A pull faster than the game's climb speed (about 1.1 m/s with full stamina, slower when tired) finishes a moment after your hand stops. With both hands on the wall, the hand that took its hold last moves you, so you can climb hand over hand. Climbing down goes in the game's own steps of about 14 cm. Out of stamina, on a surface you can only slide down, or for half a second after a lunge, the game won't climb up or down, so pulls made then are dropped and don't catch up later.
- Climb lunge: let go of your last hold at shoulder height or above while flinging the hand upward (faster than `Interaction.LungeSpeed`, 2.5 m/s), and keep it rising for a moment after. You leap that way up the wall. Grab the wall again within 0.6 s or you fall. A fast reach that catches the next hold never lunges. As in the game, a lunge needs a second since the last one and some stamina; a refused one gives the refusal cue in that hand.
- Reaching for rock: the chalk ring and the grip use the same check, so a solid ring means a grip there catches. An open hand gets the solid ring and a tick where a grip would catch now. Climbable rock just past your reach shows a faint ring, which turns solid once the hand is close enough (`Interaction.ClimbHoverRing`). With your arm held out, a grip also catches rock up to `Interaction.ClimbReachAssist` (0.2 m) past your hand along your reach, so you can grab a sloped face without leaning in. The ring sits where the grip will land, and the hold must still be within the game's reach of your head. An open hand held out over rock the game won't climb (too flat, an overhang, or past that reach) shows the red refusal ring before you grip. The grab checks the rock at your hand instead of along your view (`Interaction.ClimbGrabFromHand`). A grip that closes on a faint ring, while you reach for it or hold your arm out straight, gets the out-of-reach cue: a ring on the rock and a second ring where your reach ends. A grip closed in the 0.2 s after a climb ends waits until the game allows a new grab, then catches. A grip in open air, a pointing hand, or a hand that holds or just let go of an item gets no cue.
- Climbing feel (`Interaction.ClimbSurfaceFeel`, on): a hard catch ratchets, easy holds tick, slides grit, hazards prickle, wind shudders and a sliding vine whirrs. A rope anchor or piton that is about to fail warns in the gripping hand.
- Rope swing: lean your head back and forth with the swing to pump it higher (`Interaction.RopeSwingPump`, on). Only a clear pump counts: three quick leans back and forth, in time with the swing. Grabbing the rope, leaning in to reach it, looking around and climbing hand over hand never move it, so the rope only swings when you pump it, as in the flat game. Turning or nodding your head counts as looking around, not leaning: the pump follows your neck, not your eyes. Pumping stops adding once the swing would carry you 0.4 m out from under the rope's anchor (about 0.8 m end to end, on a rope of any length), so your view is never dragged around; a lean against the swing still slows it. That limit comes from treating the rope as a simple pendulum, so the rope's own bending can carry a swing a little past it. A rope draped over an edge already hangs away from its anchor, so it pumps less or not at all. It works on ropes you put out yourself, because only a rope's owner can swing it. On someone else's rope, the first clear pump in each hold gives the refusal cue instead.
- Gripping a rope, vine or piton the game refuses gives the refusal cue at that hand, with a ring, sound and tick that say why: not anchored, too slack, no stamina, or taken.
- An item on the ground is picked up when your eyes could see it: its nearest point, its centre or its top. An item half sunk into uneven ground still counts, but one behind a rock fin does not.
- A grab with a free hand while you hold an item puts the item in your pocket, with a pin notice once the host has taken it. With every pocket full the grab is refused with the pockets-full cue, and the item stays where it is. A pickup the host refuses gets the same cue at the hand that asked.
- Two-hand items: your free hand joins the held item when it closes on the item's second grip, or on the item's body when nothing else is at that hand. Any item the game itself holds in both hands takes a second hand, Bing Bong included, whichever hand holds it. With both hands on it, the item sits between them: the middle of its two grips stays on the middle of your hands, it lies along the line from one hand to the other, and it rolls with your wrists (turn both and it turns with them, turn one and it turns half as far). Each hand is drawn on its own grip. When your hands are farther apart or closer together than the grips, each hand is off its grip by half the difference. A hand that closes within 25 cm of the second grip takes that grip; one that closes elsewhere on the item holds it where it closed. An item with a front (Bing Bong's face, a dial, a cover) keeps showing it to you as the second hand joins. While one hand holds such an item and the free hand comes within about 45 cm of the second grip, a teal double ring marks that grip, with a chevron from your hand toward it (the same "would work here" hint as below, without a sound, and only with `UI.ActionHints` on). It goes away once that hand grips. The hand that picked the item up is its main hand: use, drop, throw and prompts stay with it. Open it while the other hand still grips the item and the item passes to that hand, with a light tick, instead of dropping. Letting go with either hand, or gripping again, moves the item onto its new hold over a quarter of a second instead of snapping it there. A rope, chain, ledge or item there takes the grab instead. Only the rescue hook and the antizooka need both hands, and with `Interaction.RequireTwoHands` off they work one-handed too, as in the flat game.
- Pointing at the scout statue with an amulet in hand, or at a skeleton with the Book of Bones, and closing the grip (index straight) runs that action from up to the game's interact reach. It wins over rock or a belt slot in reach, but something right at your hand still takes the grab. Controllers without a trigger touch sensor cannot point (see Ping): bring the amulet to its socket and grip the skeleton instead.
- When your character's arm cannot reach your controller, for example in a deep stoop, the held item leaves the character's hand and follows your controller. It goes back into the hand when the arm catches up.
- Use follows the hand that holds the item: its trigger is the primary use and the other trigger is the secondary use. With empty hands the dominant hand's trigger (right by default) climbs and the other reaches out to help a friend, like the game's mouse buttons. When both hands point, the dominant one aims the interact.
- Grip, trigger and the hand throw come from the physical interaction settings (`Interaction` section). There, `DropButton` picks the drop button. The default, `DominantSecondary`, is B on your dominant hand, so it moves to the left controller with `Input.DominantHand = Left`. Names starting with `Left` or `Right` stay on that controller whichever hand is dominant.
- Letting go: a slow grip release drops the held item (`Interaction.ReleaseToDrop`, on; off keeps the item until you throw it or press drop) and a fast one throws it (`ThrowByHand`). Your arm is much slower than the game's charged throw, so a throw grows from your hand's speed at `ThrowMinSpeed` (2 m/s) to a fully charged throw at `ThrowFullSpeed` (7 m/s, a hard swing), and a coconut thrown that hard breaks. Only an item your grip closed on can be dropped or thrown this way. An item that arrives in a hand that is already gripping, such as one you pick with the stick or a wheel, or one a friend hands you, stays put until you open and close that grip again. A grip with nothing at the hand picks up the item your head or pointing hand aims at; that item lands in the gripping hand and drops when you open it. A controller that loses tracking keeps its grip and trigger as they were, so a controller hidden behind your body or back for a few seconds never drops, throws or regrabs anything. Only a controller that is gone counts as an open hand: one the runtime reports disconnected for 2 s in a row (switched off, flat; a radio hiccup is shorter), or one untracked for 8 s. While the SteamVR dashboard or anything else holds the headset's focus, these clocks stop, so a dashboard check mid-climb in multiplayer, where the game does not pause, never drops you or your item. Then its grip opens without a throw: the held item drops (with `ReleaseToDrop` off it stays in your hand), a rope, vine or handle it held lets go, and a soft note plays at the hand.
- Toggle grip (`Interaction.GripMode`, `Hold` by default) is for weak or tired hands. In `Toggle`, a grip that closes on a wall, rope, vine, handle, a friend you lift, or an item you would drop by opening your hand (`ReleaseToDrop`) stays closed when you relax, and a tick tells you it latched. To let go, squeeze firmly again and open your hand. A softer tick follows and the hand lets go the way opening it does in `Hold`: open slowly to drop the item, fast to throw it. A grip on nothing never latches, and a hold the game ends on its own (torn off a wall, food eaten) lets the latch go.
- The game ignores a throw or drop while the held item is still in use, for example while you eat. If you throw or open your hand then, the item stays in your hand and you get the wait cue.
- The emote wheel opens centred on the hand that holds Y. Move that hand out onto an emote on the ring, point the laser at one, or push the stick, then release Y to play it. The hand in the middle of the wheel or past its edge picks nothing. Stick up and down on the right controller flips the page. Rest your thumb on Y and the game's wave picture shows just above the controller; hold Y and a ring fills around it until the wheel opens. The backpack wheel opens in front of you, about 0.75 m away toward the lower corner on the side of the hand that grips the pack; move that hand toward a pocket and let go. Letting go on nothing gives a soft cancel tick. A wheel the game refuses (for example while climbing) gives the refusal cue.
- Under SteamVR, Quest and Rift controllers send their Menu button to the SteamVR dashboard, so the game never sees it. The game opens its pause menu behind the dashboard instead (`UI.PauseOnDashboard`, on): close the dashboard and the pause menu is there. Anything else that takes input focus from the game mid-run pauses it the same way. On runtimes that take focus when you take the headset off, that pauses the game too. Recenter with both sticks instead.
- Recenter: click both sticks in and hold them for `Camera.RecenterHoldSeconds` (1 s by default). This works on every controller. Where Menu reaches the game, holding it for the same time recenters too, and letting go before then pauses. A small ring just above the controller you are holding fills while you hold (the off-hand one for the sticks). Press the two sticks within about 0.2 s of each other and neither one sprints or crouches. A single stick click reaches sprint or crouch about 0.2 s late, since PeakXR waits that long to see whether the other stick follows (one-handed play has no such wait). On a Vive wand the move hand's rim click does not walk while it is part of the chord. The Recenter button under `RecenterHoldSeconds` in the VR tab's Camera section recenters too, from any controller.
- World scale: by default (`Camera.WorldScaleMode` Auto) the world is sized to your height, measured when you recenter. Your eyes sit at your character's eyes and the game's floor sits on your real floor, so your head is as high above the ground in the game as in your room, and your hand touches the game's ground where it touches the floor. The first time a new character stands still, PeakXR measures where its eyes really are. If that moves your view by more than 2 cm, the change waits up to 3 s for a fade that comes anyway (a scene load, a respawn, a loading screen) and lands while the view is black; when none comes, the view blinks to black once to apply it, so it never jumps in front of you. Recenter standing up straight: a recenter while crouched makes the world look smaller until you recenter again (it stays between 0.75 and 1.25 of the game's size). Crouching later changes nothing. In seated mode the world is sized for a standing adult. To pick a size yourself, set `WorldScaleMode` to Manual and use `Camera.WorldScale` (0.87, from 0.6 to 1.25; lower makes everything look smaller). If you had changed `WorldScale` before, it stays and the mode starts as Manual.
- Bug report: on any controller, keep both sticks held past the recenter; the off hand feels a second hold fill, and 1.5 s later the report is saved. Where Menu reaches the game (not Quest or Rift under SteamVR), you can also hold Menu on one controller and click the other controller's stick: let go within 1.5 s to drop a marker, or keep holding for 1.5 s to save a report. Click the stick within a second of pressing Menu, before the recenter fires. Neither button does its usual job during the gesture, and only one of the two gestures runs at a time. If the view is faded (the recenter blink the sticks gesture starts), the report waits up to 2 s for it to clear, so the screenshot is not black. [docs/debugging.md](debugging.md) has the details.
- Valve Index: SteamVR keeps the system button for its dashboard. Squeeze either trackpad firmly instead; it works as the menu button.
- While you are dead or passed out you watch a teammate from a detached view behind them (`Camera.TakeoverView`). Flick the turn stick up or down to switch to the next or previous teammate; the view fades between them. `FirstPerson` puts you at the eyes instead, riding a tumbling body or a teammate's head as it moves, which can make you sick; the default `ThirdPerson` is the comfortable choice. You can also point either hand at a teammate and pull that hand's trigger to watch them. Arrows on that stick mark next and previous, and the teammate's name, status bar and items float on them (`UI.SpectateOnPlayer`).
- When you draw an item, its prompts (use, drop, scroll) appear as small labels beside the buttons that do them for 3 s, and again whenever you look at your hand (`UI.ButtonLabels`). Each label sits on a dark plate, with the button's name as large as the label text.
- When you hold something with a scroll function (rope spool, binoculars, guidebook, scout cannon), stick up and down scroll it. Put the item away to change items.
- Rope spool, paying out and setting the length: hold the spool in one hand. Twist that wrist like a knob to pay rope out or reel it in, about 30 degrees per step (`Interaction.Tools.TwistScroll`, `TwistStepDegrees`). Or close the grip of your free hand at the spool, within about 15 cm, and pull away to pay rope out, or push back to reel it in (`Interaction.Tools.PullRope`). The free hand ticks once per 0.25 m of rope. The stick scrolls too. The length you leave is the length of the rope once it is tied.
- Rope spool, tying off: point the hand that holds the spool at a wall or the ground within 10 m, with the spot no more than 5 m from your body, then hold the trigger. The game shows a ghost anchor and fills the use progress over about 2 s. Release early to cancel. When it completes, the rope is anchored there, and you release the trigger before trying again.
- Rope spool, refused: when your aim meets no wall within 10 m, or the wall is more than 5 m from your body, the trigger gives the refused cue instead of the success tick. A red ghost of the anchor and a slashed ring mark the spot, a falling note plays there and the controller ticks three times. With `UI.ActionHintText` on, the reason also shows as text ("No wall in reach", then "Aim at a closer wall").
- Target names: the thing your hand or gaze would pick up or use glows, and its name stays hidden unless you turn on `UI.TargetNames`. A plain pickup gets no picture either. Hold rings, eat and feed, struggle and refusals still show theirs. Some items, such as the berries, differ only by colour, so in colour-blind mode every item you point at shows its name. `UI.ColorBlindNames` follows the game's colour-blind setting by default (`FollowGame`); set it to `On` or `Off` to decide yourself.
- Signs (biome titles, the fog, lava and ghost banners, the ascent title, the endgame countdown and the win and lose cards) stay where they appeared in the world. A sign moves back in front of you, fading in, only after you have looked more than 45° away from it for a second, or once you have moved 20 m. A glance away and back leaves it where it is.
- Text size: `UI.TextScale` (1, from 1 to 2) enlarges the text the mod puts in the world: the target prompt, the labels by your buttons, refusal labels, banners and the wrist display. 1 is already the readable minimum, so it only grows. The VR tab picks up a new size the next time you open it. A target prompt with rock between it and your eyes fades to faint, so it never seems to float in front of the wall; smoke and particles don't fade it.
- Wrist display (`UI.HUD.HudMode` Wrist): the stamina bar sits on a dark plate the size of what it shows. The plate grows when extra stamina, a status segment or the numb mushrooms appear and shrinks back when they go, so it never leaves empty room around the bar.
- Stamina at the top of your view (`UI.HUD.StaminaView`, Wrist): `TopOfView` moves the stamina bar off the wrist to a small meter about 22° above the middle of your view, 1 m out and about 11° wide, on the same dark plate. It stays level with the horizon. Small head movements leave it where it is; look further away and a quarter of a second later it eases back into place. Rock in front of you pulls it nearer at the same size. `Both` keeps the bar on the wrist and shows a copy at the top.
- Refused actions: anything the game refuses or limits gets the same cue, whatever your settings. The thing you tried turns red (the held item, a placement ghost, the stamina bar), a ring at the spot shows a shape for the cause, a soft falling note plays there and the controller gives three firm ticks. Try again quickly and you feel the ticks again, though the ring and note wait almost a second before they repeat. The shape tells you why: a slash means no, a bar means full and a sweeping arc means wait. A bolt is stamina, two inward chevrons mean both hands, an arrow says aim higher or lower, and a chevron onto a line asks for solid ground. A person points at friends, a padlock means locked or not here, an empty cup means used up or not lit, a weight is too heavy, wavy lines are spoiled food and "!" warns of danger. When the refusal is about another player, you see the ring only and their body keeps its colour. A refused emote wheel, pause, greyed-out control, locked cosmetic, end of a book and keyboard key all give the same cue. A control that greys out under a laser you hold still, like an arrow at its last step, reads as locked from the next press. A ring whose spot is out of sight (a hand overhead on a rope, something beside or behind you) also shows a copy at the edge of your view, about 1 m away, with a pointer toward the real spot. The real ring stays where it is. Notices (an item stowed or dropped, friends revived, a cannon about to fire) use a pin in a teal ring and a soft bell. Hold-to-use targets show a ring that fills while you hold, on the hand doing it: the hand holding the item, the pointing or gripping hand, the free hand while the other holds something, else your dominant hand. Target verbs show as button pictures; `UI.ActionHintText` brings the words back. `UI.ActionHints`, on by default, also shows where the action would work: a teal double ring, a chevron moving toward it, a rising note and the reach ray. Turning it off hides those hints; refusals still cue. The first time each kind of refusal happens, its short reason also shows as text beside the ring, so you can learn what the shape means. After that you get the shape alone (`UI.FirstTimeRefusalLabels`, on). The Reset button under that setting in the VR tab shows every reason's text once more. With `UI.ActionHintText` on, the text shows every time. With `Interaction.Haptics` off, the controller stops vibrating for everything, menu ticks included, while the rings and notes still play. `UI.CueVolume` (1, from 0 to 2) sets how loud the cue notes are, without changing any other game sound.
- Vibration strength: `Interaction.HapticStrength` sets the strength of every vibration, gameplay and menus alike. 1 is normal and the default, 2 is twice as strong and 0 is off. A config saved by an older version moves over on the first start: the old default 0.5 becomes 1, and any other value is doubled (up to 2), so it feels the same as before. Refusals and danger warnings are felt stronger than a menu hover tick at any strength setting. Each hand plays one vibration at a time, by priority: refusals and danger warnings first, then feedback on what the hand did (a grab, contact, progress, a use), then background feel. A vibration never cuts off one of higher priority; a grab or contact tick that comes during a refusal is skipped. Background feel (heartbeat, wind, surface texture, shiver, warmth) pauses while any other cue plays and for 0.1 s after. A hold's progress ticks are the exception: they pause it only while each tick plays, so a fire's rumble keeps going while you cook or light something. `Interaction.HapticDetail` set to `Essential` turns background feel off and keeps everything else; `Full` is the default. Climbing stamina warnings are not background feel: the quickening pulse on low stamina, the beat when it is gone and the slide buzz all play at `Essential`, and while you hang on a wall with no stamina the beat replaces the rumble of the game's camera shake. A hold that has its own growing buzz (a suitcase lid, the Book of Bones, a cursed item at your face, a hold on a friend) skips the hold ring's quarter ticks and gives only the final tick. Other holds, like eating or a piton, keep the quarter ticks.
- Binoculars: raise them with the trigger of the hand that holds them. By default (`Interaction.Binoculars.Activation = Toggle`) they stay up until you pull the trigger again, as in the game. With `Hold` they stay up only while you hold the trigger and lower when you let go. Twist the wrist to zoom (`Interaction.Binoculars.TwistZoom`). Twisting past the end of the zoom gives one hard thunk, then nothing more. The zoomed view, looking where the binoculars point, shows on a small lens at their eyepieces; bring the eyepieces to your eyes (`Interaction.Binoculars.EyeDistance`) and it fills the view in front of you. The binoculars lying in the airport zoom too in VR, though in the flat game they only rise to the face.
- Vine and chain shooters: when the game finds no clear path for the vine to where you aimed, nothing is built and the refusal cue marks that spot. The warp compass gives the friends cue at your hand when nobody is far enough away to warp to, and the game gives the use back.
- WMR controllers have no A/B buttons: click the top of the touchpad for B/Y and the bottom for A/X.
- Vive wands: the trackpad only acts when clicked, so a resting thumb never walks or turns. The off-hand pad walks toward your thumb while clicked (click at the rim to sprint). On the dominant pad, click left or right to turn, the top for B/Y, the bottom for A/X and the centre for the stick click. Hotbar and scroll flicks have no pad: use the belt slots and the wrist-twist gestures. A click in the middle of the off-hand pad is that hand's B/Y: tap it to ping, hold it for the emote wheel. A click that starts in the middle and slides out ends B/Y without pinging and walks from there (so a rim click aimed for the both-sticks recenter still works), and a click that starts outside the middle never turns into B/Y. The off hand has no A/X: open the backpack over your shoulder and put items away on the belt.
- Khronos Simple controllers (select and menu only): hold select to grip, which climbs and grabs. They have no stick, trigger or face buttons, so this profile is a fallback that won't let you walk, jump or use items.
- Talk: hold a free hand at your mouth like a walkie-talkie. A click and a mic icon by the hand show the mic is open (`Voice.HandToMouthTalk`, on). It only matters when the game's microphone mode is Push To Talk, and only while the controller is tracked: a controller that loses tracking at your mouth closes the mic. Cover your mouth with a flat open palm to mute (`Voice.PalmMute`, on). No button talks by default: every button already has a job. To use one, set `Voice.PushToTalkButton`; that control is then reserved for talking.
- Ping: make a finger gun (index out, thumb lifted off the stick, the buttons, the Touch thumbrest and the Index trackpad), or rest a thumb on B/Y. Controllers that cannot feel a finger on the trigger (Vive wands, WMR and Reverb G2) have no finger gun and no pointed grip, since an index off the trigger cannot be told from one resting on it: ping with B/Y (on a Vive wand, a click in the middle of the off-hand pad). In one-handed play these controllers have no ping. A finger gun never picks anything up. Pointing with the thumb resting on the stick aims that hand's interaction instead. A dot and a dotted line show where the ping will land. A light pull on the trigger keeps them; only the full squeeze pings, and opening the grip ends the aim. When the ping would land on nothing, the dot in the sky turns red with a slashed ring around it, and pinging there gets the refusal cue.
- Emotes by gesture (`Emotes.GestureEmotes`, on): wave a raised open hand to wave, hold both open hands overhead to cheer, or hold an empty fist still out in front of you, at chest height or above, with the thumb up for a thumbs-up. Gestures do nothing while you climb, hang on a rope or vine, carry a friend, or grip something that took the grip. They also do nothing while you are passed out or dead, in a menu, or with a wheel open. The thumbs-up also needs a controller that senses your thumb, so a plain fist never sends one.
- Friends: pinch a thorn or arrow, then pull your hand back to pluck it (`Social.PullToPluck`, on); without the setting the grip alone starts it. In a custom run with the helping hand set to kick, push both open palms at a friend to kick them (`Social.ShoveKick`, on). Gripping a friend never kicks. While a friend or condor carries you, or you stand on a crouched friend, your hands feel a light sway or pressure (`Social.CarriedSway`, `Social.PalJumpFeel`, both on).
- An open passport or guidebook sits above the hand that holds it, its lower edge clear of the hand, so the hand never covers a page. A hand held low or close lifts it to at most 35° below your eyes and at least 0.3 m out. The trigger that opened the passport closes it again (unless that hand's laser is on the passport). Turn pages by swiping your other hand across it. The passport's tabs and page buttons also take the laser, and the guidebook also turns with stick up and down.
- Menus (title, pause, settings, passport, end screen) sit on a dark rounded plate cut to the buttons and text they hold. It grows or shrinks when a page, tab or open list changes, and it never takes a click. `UI.MenuBackplate` (on) switches it off and `UI.MenuBackplateOpacity` (0.85) sets how dark it can get. At 0.85, white text stays readable even against snow in sunlight. With `UI.MenuBackplateAdaptive` (on) each plate, the VR tab's column, category rail and preview card included, follows what is behind it as seen from your eyes, measured in turn, one plate every half second, so with the VR tab open each plate is measured about every 2 s: as dark as that setting over bright snow or sky, lighter over a dark background, never under 0.4. It darkens within a fraction of a second when the background brightens and lightens over about a second. A menu whose buttons and text spread over more than 70° of your view, like the title, gets no plate, since one plate around all of it would be a full-view screen. In the VR tab, dropdown text is black or white, whichever is easier to read on its box.
- Menus and popups follow you lazily (`UI.MenuFollow`, LazyFollow). A menu stays where it opened while you look around it. Once you look more than `UI.MenuFollowAngle` (30°, 15 to 60) away from its centre for half a second, or past the edge of a wide menu such as the title screen, it glides back in front of you, starting and stopping gently. It never moves while the laser is on a menu or a trigger is held. `Fixed` leaves every menu where it opened. The passport in your hand and the kiosk panels stay where they are.
- A popup that blacks out its page on a monitor, such as "Leave the expedition?" over the pause menu, hides that page in VR too, so only the popup and its own plate show. As on a monitor, pointing where the hidden buttons were does nothing.
- Menu panels at a kiosk (boarding pass, custom run options) and the end-of-run results take the laser like any other menu. At the end of a won run, any controller button skips the credits wait, the same as any key on a keyboard.

## One-handed play

Set `Accessibility.OneHanded` to `Left` or `Right` to play with that controller alone. The other controller is ignored, even if it is still switched on, and the layout below replaces the two-controller one. `Input.DominantHand` stops mattering while it is on.

| Control | The one controller |
|---|---|
| Stick | Up/down: walk forward or back (toward your head or your hand, per `Locomotion.MoveRelativeTo`). Left/right: turn. A push counts as whichever is larger, so it never walks and turns at once |
| Stick click | Sprint. Keep it pressed for half a second to crouch as well |
| A / X | Jump |
| B / Y | Tap: pause. Hold: emote wheel |
| Grip | Grab or interact. Open it to drop, swing and open it to throw |
| Trigger | Use. With the stick clicked in as you pull it: the secondary use (use on a friend, reach out with an empty hand) |
| Menu | Release within 1 s: pause. Hold 1 s: recenter. Controllers without one (the Touch right controller) pause on B and recenter with the Recenter button under `RecenterHoldSeconds` in the VR tab's Camera section |

The bug-report gesture needs both controllers, so one-handed play saves reports and markers from the VR tab's Debug section instead ([docs/debugging.md](debugging.md)).

On a Vive wand the trackpad stands in for the stick and the buttons, and acts only when clicked:

- Top half: walk forward. At the rim: sprint as well.
- Bottom half: walk back. At the rim: B, so tap it to pause and hold it for the emote wheel.
- Left or right side: turn.
- Centre: tap to jump, hold for half a second to crouch.
- Menu button: tap to pause, hold to recenter. A long press opens no wheel here, because the hold already recenters.

What moves off the buttons:

- Hotbar: draw from and holster on the belt slots. Stick up and down walks now, so it no longer cycles the hotbar or scrolls a held item. Twist your wrist instead: that pays out rope and tilts the scout cannon (`Interaction.Tools.TwistScroll`) and zooms the binoculars (`Interaction.Binoculars.TwistZoom`).
- Put away: holster the item on the belt. Backpack: reach over a shoulder, onto its top or just behind it, and grip to open its wheel, or hold that grip still in the wheel's centre for a second to take the pack off.
- Ping: make a finger gun with the empty hand.
- Crouch: crouch for real (`Locomotion.PhysicalCrouch`), or keep the stick clicked in.
- Drop: open the grip (`Interaction.ReleaseToDrop`). With `ReleaseToDrop` off, `Interaction.DropButton` drops as usual and takes B over from pause and the emote wheel.

Everything that needs both hands takes one:

- A respawn chest opens with one hand lifting its lid.
- A two-handed item (the antizooka, rescue hook) works in one hand.
- Cheer: hold one open hand still over your head for about a second. A hand that swings is a wave.
- The kick shove: push one open palm at a friend.
- Soul pillar: lay one palm on it.
- Lifting a passed-out friend takes one grip (as with `Social.CarryWithBothHands` off).
- The wrist display moves to the one controller's wrist.

## VR Guide and practice

In VR the VR tab of the settings (title or pause menu) is laid out for the headset. The settings of the chosen category stand in one column about 30° wide; push the stick up or down while pointing at it, or drag it with the laser, to scroll. The categories are a small panel of their own to the left of the column, turned toward you, that scrolls the same way, and each panel has its own plate. Every vibration setting is in the Haptics category, under a heading for the part of the game it belongs to, and the item and fire gestures have their own Items category. The config file keeps every setting where it was. Text is at least 1.3° tall wherever the menu stands. The game's own tabs keep the game's layout.

The VR tab uses the game's own settings pieces, in VR and on a monitor. Section headings look like the headings on the game's settings page. Buttons are copies of the settings page's Back button: red for Back, Stop and Previous page, green for every other action (Play, Start, Save bug report, Reset). Click the button itself; resting the laser anywhere on a demo row still plays its demo. On the Compatibility page a working feature reads white, a failed one shows in the game's warning red and one that is off is dimmed. The card beside a row is white text like the rest of the menu, and the preview shapes use the game's gold accent, the colour of its slider handle.

In VR the VR tab of the settings (title or pause menu) starts with a VR Guide section. It covers only what PeakXR adds or changes in VR; PEAK's own rules and items are left to the game.

- Demos: press Show to list one row per VR mechanic: grab and drop, two-hand hold, throw, eat, belt holster, backpack, wall climb, rope and vine, piton, cook, light a fire, open a suitcase, bugle, bandage, parasol, turning, walk and sprint, crouch, emote wheel, ping, talk, mute, spectate, pause and recenter, menus and laser, wrist status and refused actions. Rest the laser on a row or click it and a small ghost of you acts it out beside the panel for two loops. Its controllers light the button it presses, the rings and notes are the same cues the game uses in play (a double ring and a rising note where an action works, a slashed ring and a falling note where it is refused), and the matching real controller ticks. With `UI.ActionHintText` on, a one-line caption sits under the ghost. The list also has rows for the altar socket, release to drop, refusals out of view and their first label, rope pumping, the respawn chest, ground pickups, helping up, carrying, feeding, shaking off a web, hammering a piton, glider steering and giving up.
- Practice: press Start. The tab first shows only the comfort choices (turn mode, vignette strength, seated mode) with their usual previews. Press Start again to go: from the title it plays solo offline and walks you through stations in the airport. In the solo airport it starts where you are. The stations come in four zones, the most important nearest:
  - Basics, where you start: turn, hold both sticks in to recenter, grab an apple, then point at an apple out of reach to feel a refusal.
  - Body, a few steps on: holster the apple on your belt, eat, throw.
  - Climbing, at the airport's climbing wall: climb the wall, then the rope hanging from it.
  - Social, a short walk past that: open the emote wheel.

  Before each new zone, a beam of light stands over the spot, a double ring lies on the floor there, the next demo already plays beside it, and a note sounds from it every few seconds so you can find it by ear. Walk into the ring and the station starts. A zone that the scene has no place for goes to the nearest open floor a short walk out, and the log says which one it used. Each station shows its demo at its zone and marks where to act with a double ring. When you do it, a rising note plays, both controllers pulse and a ring widens. Two orbs float at chest height on your off-hand side: rest a hand in the chevron orb to skip a station, or in the cross orb to leave. The chevron during a walk skips the walk: the zone comes to where you stand, except the climbing zone, which needs its wall. While it runs, the Practice row in the VR tab reads Stop. Finishing, leaving and stopping all end back at the title. Boarding the plane ends the practice and keeps the run you started.
- The practice never runs online. In an online lobby, or in a solo run in progress, Start plays the refused cue at the row (the "Not here, start it from the title" label with `UI.ActionHintText` on) and does nothing.
- The first time you reach the title in VR, a small offer appears once beside the title panel: a ghost reaching for an apple and two orbs within reach. Rest a hand in the chevron orb to open the comfort-first practice view, or in the cross orb to dismiss it. It also goes away when you pick anything in the menu, and the Practice row stays in the guide for later. Until you choose one of the two orbs, the offer comes back the next time you start the game. Both orbs are plain white, so they don't look like the teal and red of the action cues.

## Item room

With `Sandbox.Enabled` on, the Sandbox page of the VR tab has an Item room row: Enter or leave. It opens the item room in the solo airport (`docs/testing.md`), every item on waist-high tables by kind: helpful ones nearest you, dangerous ones behind them, dynamite farthest out. Rest a hand in the white chevron orb at a table to restock that table, in the chevron orb in front of where you started to restock everything, in the "Light fire" orb (left of the restock-everything orb) to light the stove, or in the cross orb beside the restock orb to go back to the title. In the solo airport the room always stands in the same place in the hall, just out from the spawn elevators. If you press Enter elsewhere, the bell rings from it and a note in front of your eyes says how far away it is. If no open floor nearby is big enough (about 12 x 12 m), you get the refusal cue and a note in front of your eyes with the size it found.

## Items and fires by hand

Each of these has an on/off setting in the `Interaction` section. With a setting off, the grip and trigger work as before.

An empty hand is drawn where your controller is: the character's arm is drawn longer, up to one and a half times, when its shoulder sits too low and far back for its own arm to get there. A held item stays in your character's hand. When your real hand goes further than that, such as a deep stoop, a long reach or a hand holding something past the arm's reach, a faint ghost hand shows where your hand really is (`Avatar.ReachGhostHand`, on). Only you see both.

- Cook (`CookOverFire`): hold food over a lit campfire. It cooks while it stays above the flames and stops when you pull it out. Held near a lit fire, food gives a faint pulse that gets stronger toward the heat. Over the flames a soft buzz takes over at that pulse's strength and grows as it cooks. Food that cannot be used on the fire, or a fire that is not lit, gets the refusal cue.
- Open a suitcase (`LiftLidToOpen`): grip the top of the case and lift. The game's open timer only runs as far as you have raised the lid, so lift slowly. A respawn chest needs both hands on the lid; one hand alone gets the two-hands cue. Gripping the case low down holds it open the old way, and shows an upward ring at the lid edge to say where to lift.
- Bugle (`BugleAtMouth`): put its mouthpiece to your mouth to play (the hand holds it lower down). Tilt the bell up for a higher note and down for a lower one. The trigger plays it too.
- Light things (`LightWithFlame`): touch a lit torch, flare, candle or lantern to a cannon or rocket fuse, a ghost fire or an unlit campfire and keep it there until it catches.
- Food and drinks: touch them to your mouth (the item itself counts, not your fist). A drink is drunk from its opening: put the neck, cap or spout to your lips. Its base or side at your cheek or chin does nothing. Pulling them away before the use finishes cancels it. The trigger alone doesn't eat or drink: it gets the refusal cue and a ring at your mouth. For seated or one-handed play, turn on `TriggerEats` and the trigger eats and drinks too.
- Feeding a friend: hold the food to their mouth and pull the trigger. Pulling it just short of their mouth gets the out-of-reach cue, with the ring on their mouth. An item they can't eat gets the can't-use cue at their mouth, even if the item has a use of its own.
- Bandages, heat packs, chalk and other healing items (`PressToBody`): press the item to your chest or your other forearm to use it. Press it to a friend's body to use it on them. The hand has to slow down at the body: sweeping the item past your chest, as in a throw, doesn't start it.
- Book of Bones (`ReadAtFace`, on): hold it open in front of your eyes to read it. Lowering it stops.
- Cursed items (`CommuneAtFace`, on): they only work held eye to eye for the whole use. The trigger alone is refused with a warning.
- Revive with the Book of Bones (`BookOverBones`, on): hold the open book just above the skeleton's hips and keep it there. Gripping the skeleton still works.
- Summit altar (`AmuletInSocket`, on): bring an amulet to its socket. The Peak altar's sockets sit out of reach, so hold the amulet up over your head under the socket, or point at the statue and close the grip. Pointing at it with an amulet shows the socket it will fill, and a pointed grip without an amulet is refused. `GuardWastedItems` (on) refuses two actions the game would waste: an amulet into a full altar, and a scout effigy when nobody is down.
- Soul pillar (`PalmsOnPillar`, on): lay both open palms on it.
- Summit portal (`HandIntoPortal`, on): push a hand into it to enter, and pull it out to stop.
- A cannon about to fire, a checkpoint that saved you, a summoned helicopter and the last ten seconds of the summit countdown are felt as ticks.
- Fire rumble (`WarmHandsAtFire`): a hand near a lit campfire's flames feels a continuous crackling rumble. It starts about a hand's width (0.3 m) outside the flames, grows smoothly as the hand closes in, and is at full strength just under the flames' surface and anywhere in them. This changes nothing in the game.
- Parasol (`ParasolRunner`): grip the shaft with your free hand and push up to open it, or pull down to close it.

## Turning and comfort

Settings live in the `Comfort` section. `Preset` sets several of them at once:

| Preset | Turning | Vignette | Move smoothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comfortable | Snap 30° | Strong | Off (instant) |
| Moderate (default) | Snap 30°; smooth turn, if chosen, at 60°/s | Medium | Off (instant) |
| Intense | Smooth 150°/s | Off | Off |

Seated reach (`Comfort.SeatedReachGain`, 2, from 1 to 3; 1 turns it off): in seated mode, a hand lowered below your hips reaches further down, so a controller near the real floor reaches the ground in game for pickups, lids and floor gestures. A higher value gets there sooner.

Changing `TurnMode`, `SnapTurnAngle`, `SmoothTurnSpeed`, `Vignette`, `VignetteStrength` or `MoveSmoothing` yourself switches the preset to Custom, unless the values then match a preset. A preset only counts the settings it uses: `SnapTurnAngle` only with snap turning, `SmoothTurnSpeed` only with smooth turning, and `VignetteStrength` only with the vignette on. If you edit `Preset` in the config file, the game applies that preset at the next start, unless you also edited the values so they match no preset; then the preset becomes Custom. The vignette darkens the edge of your view while your character moves, falls or smooth-turns without you moving, and while the view follows a body you don't control (spectating, passed out, carried). It starts closing at `VignetteStartSpeed` (0.2 m/s, so any artificial motion shows it) and reaches `VignetteStrength` at `VignetteFullSpeed` (2.5 m/s, about walking speed). `MoveSmoothing` sets how many seconds the move stick takes to reach full speed. At 0 it is instant, which is the most comfortable: a ramp stretches out the acceleration that causes motion sickness. The detached view (spectating, passed out, a tumble or launch) never glides fast: it speeds up and slows down gently and stops where the body stops. A body that gets away is caught up behind a fade. Choosing Comfortable also sets `Camera.ThirdPersonFollow` to 0, so the detached view stays still and jumps to a new spot behind a fade. Moderate and Intense set it back to 0.8. A preset changes it only while it still holds the previous preset's value, so a value you set yourself stays, and changing it never switches the preset to Custom. A config that still holds a preset's old values moves to the new ones, and values you changed yourself stay. A Custom config still on the old 120°/s smooth turn speed moves to 60°/s only if you snap turn, so a speed you have been playing with stays. `SeatedMode` treats your seated head height as standing height.

## Moving

Settings live in the `Locomotion` section.

- `MoveRelativeTo`: the move stick walks where you look (`Head`) or where the move hand points (`Hand`).
- `SprintMode` and `CrouchMode`: `Toggle` (click once) or `Hold`.
- `PhysicalCrouch` (on): crouch for real to crouch in game. It triggers when your head drops by `CrouchDepth` (0.2, a fifth of the standing height measured at your last recenter; 0.1 to 0.45) and lets go once you rise back above 70 % of that drop. At the default, stooping to the ground crouches the character, so its hands reach where yours are, while a nod or a glance at the floor does not. Your view stays at your real head. Seated mode never triggers it, and the crouch click keeps working. If you sit down mid-run and keep moving while crouched for 10 s, a notice at your off-hand wrist tells you: stand up, or hold both sticks in for 1 s to recenter. In one-handed play it says to hold Menu for 1 s or use Recenter in the VR tab instead.
- `ArmSwingSprint` (on): swing your arms like running while the move stick walks forward to sprint. The stick still decides where you go and how fast you walk; swinging alone never moves you. Only a swing below the chest counts, and gestures made standing still never sprint. It stays off while you climb or hold an item with both hands.
- `HopToJump` (off): swing both arms up and hop to jump. Standing up from a crouch, looking down and running arms do not jump.
- Walking around your play space moves your character with you. Your view stays where your head is and the character steps after it once you lean further than `LeanLimit` (0.35 m); a smaller lean bends the character's upper body instead. After standing back up from a lean past the limit, the character stays where it stepped and only straightens up. `LeanLimit` replaced `LeanDistance` (0.2 m), which walked the character on every lean; an old `LeanDistance` line left in the config file is ignored and can be deleted. If the character cannot follow, for example against a wall or while climbing, the view fades out and back in centred on the character once you get more than 1 m away from it, or once its steps after you stall (less than 3 cm closer in 1.5 s). Before that fade, a "not now" ring at the character's feet shows where to come back to. Turn `RoomScaleWalking` off to skip the stepping and keep only the fade. If your head goes into rock or a wall, the view goes black until you pull it out. Once the view is fully black, a small teal ring stays in the middle of it, with a chevron pointing toward open space (up is ahead, down is behind), and a soft note sounds from that side. After a warp, revive or respawn that lands your eyes inside rock, the ring shows alone, without the chevron, until the view clears by itself.

## Game events

In VR the game never moves your view by itself. Each kind of event that would move or flash it gets a treatment instead, set in the `ComfortEvents` section:

| Setting | Event | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `Knockback` | Hits, bonks, blasts | Vignette pulse |
| `Launch` | Cannon, geyser, bad mushroom, super jump, tornado | Third-person view until you land |
| `Ragdoll` | Your body starts tumbling | Vignette pulse (a config still on the old default, Rumble, moves over once). The view itself also steps out behind the tumbling body, unless you turn off `Camera.DetachOnRagdoll` |
| `ForcedLook` | Something turns your character (frog tongue, grabs) | Rumble |
| `Shake` | Camera shake | Rumble |
| `StatusFlash` | Damage, cold, poison and other status flashes | None |
| `PostEffect` | Sugar rush, energy drink, heat, petrify screen effects | None |
| `Teleport` | Your body jumps somewhere without a run transition | Fade |
| `WindPush` | Wind, push zones, lava bounce | Vignette pulse |

Each one accepts `None`, `VignettePulse`, `FadeHoldFade`, `HapticPulse` or `ThirdPerson`. `Intensity` scales every vignette pulse and rumble, and 0 turns them off. With `Haptics` on, hits, launches, falls and shakes also rumble, whatever their treatment. Rumbles come at most every 0.08 s, and a hit that lands in between plays at the next one. The big world events that shake the camera (rising lava, a freed soul, the summit portal) are shakes too: their rumble follows `Intensity` and plays when `Shake` is `HapticPulse` or `Haptics` is on, at a strength of its own set by `UI.RunFlow.ShakeHaptics` (0.5, from 0 to 1; 0 turns it off).

`WindPressure` (on) adds a continuous gusting rumble that follows the force the wind or a tornado's pull really puts on you, stronger on the hand facing it; it fades out behind shelter. Other body feel is on top of that: a heartbeat while you spend stamina low on the bar, a fine shivering rumble as you get colder (night, fog, snowstorm), a slow swelling rumble as you get hotter (desert sun, lava; a parasol or sunscreen keeps it quiet), a throbbing rumble in a hazard zone such as a spore cloud, each as strong as that source is acting on you (it keeps rumbling with the status bar full, and stays quiet while the source itself is held off: parasol or sunscreen in the sun, shelter from wind chill), and feels for a lava sting, a bounce, a balloon, a parachute, a sugar crash and a bridge. Amulet charge shows as beads on the wrist. A head within 30 cm of rock gives a soft tick in the hand on that side. The heartbeat, the cold, heat and hazard rumbles and the rock tick each have their own switch in the `Locomotion` section (`Heartbeat`, `Shiver`, `Heat`, `HazardZones`, `WallTouch`, all on). Fire, wind, cold, heat and hazards are one unbroken rumble in each hand: they add up without passing full strength, each fades in and out smoothly, and any other vibration (a refusal, a grab, a heartbeat) plays over it, after which the rumble carries on.

`CutsceneView` picks how the end cutscene looks. `Viewpoint` (the default) puts you at the cutscene camera, facing where it looks, and you can look around. When the shot cuts or moves far, the view fades to the new spot. `FollowCamera` glides after the camera instead. That is motion you did not make, so you have to turn it on. `Screen` plays the cutscene on a cinema screen. `Mirrors` off hides mirror reflections, which in VR are drawn from a point between your eyes and look flat.
